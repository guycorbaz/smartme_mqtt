\chapter{Glossary}
\label{app:glossary}

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=0pt]
  \item[Sparkplug~B] An MQTT payload/topic specification with a stateful session lifecycle
        (BIRTH/DATA/DEATH) and native quality semantics, consumed by Ignition's MQTT Engine.
  \item[NBIRTH / NDATA / NDEATH] Node birth / data / death messages of a Sparkplug session.
  \item[LWT] MQTT Last-Will and Testament — the message the broker publishes on the
        bridge's behalf when it disconnects, driving \textsc{stale}-on-death.
  \item[\texttt{ValueDate}] The measurement timestamp reported by smart-me for a reading.
  \item[STALE] A quality flag telling the SCADA a value can no longer be trusted as fresh.
  \item[\texttt{bdSeq}] The Sparkplug birth/death sequence number, persisted across restarts.
  \item[CONTRACT\_VERSION] The version of the MQTT/Sparkplug contract embedded in the payload.
\item[Traced drop] A reading \prog accepted and then could not deliver --- a full outbox, a
  refused publish, a broker that went away mid-flight. It is \textbf{counted, by meter and by
  reason}, and reported on \code{/healthz} and on the meter page. The name is the rule: \prog
  may lose a reading, and may never lose one silently.

\item[Culprit] Whose fault a fault is: \textbf{world}, \textbf{you} or \textbf{bridge}. It
  answers \emph{who can repair this} and travels with the gesture that \emph{this} cause asks
  for. No cause the oracles produce ever accuses the bridge --- they judge readings, and a
  reading is never \prog's fault; \emph{bridge} appears only for a traced drop.

\item[Latched fault] A fault \prog does not retry, because retrying would lie: a refused
  credential, a device the account does not have, a serial that is not the one the device
  reports. The meter stays refused until the configuration or the credential changes and the
  bridge restarts. The alternative --- retrying quietly --- publishes nothing while looking
  like a transient outage.

\item[Re-arm] Restoring a guard to its ready state after it has fired, so that the next
  occurrence is caught rather than swallowed by the first. Used of the staleness watch and of
  the rate-limit wait.

\item[Reading age] How old a \emph{measurement} is, as against how long ago \prog last
  published it. \prog publishes a verdict every cycle, so a fresh publication instant beside an
  old reading age is a meter that has stopped measuring --- the pair is what makes the two
  distinguishable at all.

\item[Priority meter] A meter you marked as one that matters. It changes nothing about what is
  published; it is counted on the state screen so that, during an incident, whether the meters
  you mind about are affected is one glance rather than an inventory.
\end{description}
