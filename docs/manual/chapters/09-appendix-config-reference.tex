\chapter{Configuration reference}
\label{app:configref}

Every environment variable and every \code{config.toml} key: name, whether required,
default, and what happens if it is missing. The reasoning behind each lives in
chapter~\ref{ch:config}; this is the lookup table.

\section{The environment}

Four are honoured --- the credential pair, the state directory and \code{RUST\_LOG} --- plus
two reserved names the bridge does not read. Everything else \prog has is in
\code{config.toml}; see section~\ref{sec:configfile}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\ttfamily}l l p{6.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\normalfont\textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Req.} & \textbf{Effect} \\
\midrule
SMARTME\_CLIENT\_ID     & yes & smart-me OAuth client-credentials ID. Missing: \prog refuses to publish and says so on the screen. \\
SMARTME\_CLIENT\_SECRET & yes & smart-me OAuth client-credentials secret. Missing: \prog refuses to publish and says so on the screen. \\
\addlinespace
SMARTME\_STATE\_DIR     & no  & Where \code{config.toml} and \code{bdseq.toml} live. Default \code{/data}. \\
\addlinespace
RUST\_LOG               & no  & \code{tracing} filter, console and file alike. Default \code{info}. Stays in the environment deliberately: raising the level is what one does to diagnose a configuration that will not load, so it must be readable before the configuration is. \\
\addlinespace
SMARTME\_BASIC\_USER    & --- & HTTP Basic fallback user. \textbf{Not read by \prog}\textsuperscript{*}. \\
SMARTME\_BASIC\_PASSWORD& --- & HTTP Basic fallback password. \textbf{Not read by \prog}\textsuperscript{*}. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\footnotesize\textsuperscript{*}\textbf{The Basic fallback is not reachable from the
environment.} \code{smart-me-client} implements it (ADR~0009), but the binary constructs
OAuth client-credentials unconditionally and never builds Basic credentials, so setting
these two variables has no effect --- and because the OAuth pair is required, omitting it in
their favour makes \prog refuse to publish. Wiring it up, or dropping it from the design, is an
open decision.\normalsize

\begin{warning}
\textbf{Eleven variables were withdrawn on 2026-08-04} and setting them does nothing:
\code{SMARTME\_GROUP\_ID}, \code{SMARTME\_NODE\_ID}, \code{SMARTME\_BROKER\_HOST},
\code{SMARTME\_BROKER\_PORT}, \code{SMARTME\_METER\_ID}, \code{SMARTME\_DEVICE\_ID},
\code{SMARTME\_SERIAL}, \code{SMARTME\_PUBLISH\_PERIOD\_SECS}, \code{SMARTME\_API\_BASE},
\code{SMARTME\_LOG\_DIR}, \code{SMARTME\_LOG\_KEEP}. Each is a key in the table below.
\end{warning}

\section{\code{config.toml}}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\ttfamily}l l p{6.1cm}@{}}
\toprule
\normalfont\textbf{Key} & \textbf{Req.} & \textbf{Effect} \\
\midrule
schema\_version      & yes & Currently \code{4}. Another value is refused: there is no migration, and reading an unknown layout by guesswork would start \prog on settings nobody wrote. \\
\addlinespace
group\_id            & yes & Sparkplug group. \textbf{No default}: a host persists what it discovers, so the wrong namespace leaves a tag folder that outlives the process and must be deleted by hand. \\
node\_id             & yes & Sparkplug edge node. Same reason. \\
\addlinespace
broker\_host         & yes & Host or IP of the MQTT broker. \\
broker\_port         & no  & Default \code{1883}. A value that is not a port number is refused rather than silently replaced. \\
\addlinespace
publish\_period\_secs & no & Seconds between publishes. Default \code{30}, minimum \code{5}, maximum \code{300}. There is deliberately no value meaning ``off'': the periodic publish is what lets a host that restarted notice a node whose BIRTH it never saw and ask for a rebirth. \\
\addlinespace
api\_base            & no  & smart-me endpoint. Default \code{https://api.smart-me.com}. Must be \code{https}; a plaintext base is refused. \\
\addlinespace
log\_dir             & no  & Directory for the log file. Unset: no file at all, console only. Set: \code{smartme\_mqtt.<date>.log}, rotated daily. \\
log\_keep            & no  & Rotated files kept. Default \code{7}. Not a positive number: refused on stderr, keeps 7. \\
\addlinespace
ui\_port             & no  & Port the embedded web UI listens on \emph{inside} the container. Default \code{8080}. The container publishes no host port --- Traefik reaches it over a shared Docker network and is the only thing that can. Changing it takes effect at the next restart --- the listener is started once. It does \emph{not} disturb the Sparkplug session. \code{0} is refused, and so is anything below \code{1024}: the bridge runs unprivileged and could never listen there, and since this screen is the only place the value can be changed, accepting it would remove the only way to undo it. \\
\addlinespace
mapping\_confirmed   & no  & \textbf{Set this by hand on a first run, or nothing is ever published.} Whether a human has checked the meter→topic mapping. \textbf{Absent means \code{false}}, and nothing is published until it is \code{true} --- no connection, no birth. Editing the mapping withdraws it automatically --- and so does editing \code{group\_id} or \code{node\_id}, which are in every topic; editing anything else does not. Writing the file by hand and setting it counts as confirming it. See section~\ref{sec:confirm}. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection*{\code{[[meters]]} --- one table per meter}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\ttfamily}l l p{6.1cm}@{}}
\toprule
\normalfont\textbf{Key} & \textbf{Req.} & \textbf{Effect} \\
\midrule
meter\_id  & yes & Logical name. It becomes the metric path a SCADA host displays. \\
device\_id & yes & The smart-me device UUID behind it. \\
serial     & yes & The meter's serial. It becomes the Sparkplug device topic level, so a value that cannot be a topic level is refused. \\
enabled    & yes & Whether this meter is published. Disabling one publishes a \code{DDEATH}; enabling one publishes a \code{DBIRTH}. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{note}
The model holds any number of meters; \textbf{the runtime currently serves one}. Enabling
more than that is \emph{refused} at startup rather than silently truncated --- a bridge
that quietly published three of your four meters would be indistinguishable, from the
outside, from one meter having failed.
\end{note}

\begin{note}
Any key not in these tables is refused. That is the opposite of the usual default, and it
is the point: an ignored unknown key means a rename reads as \emph{absent}, takes its
default, and \prog runs on a configuration nobody chose. See
section~\ref{sec:configfile}.
\end{note}
