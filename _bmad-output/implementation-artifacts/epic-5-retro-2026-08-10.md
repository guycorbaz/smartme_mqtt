## What worked, and must be kept

**The ADR 0021 split — the model here, the screens elsewhere.** Every acceptance criterion in
Epic 5 is testable without a line of HTML. That is what allowed ADR 0019's secrets rule to be
falsified before a form existed, and it is why the epic could be finished while Epic 6 had not
started.

**The compiler as the thing that asks the question.** `reconfigure::classify`'s exhaustive
destructure broke the build when `ui_port` was added in story 6.1, forcing somebody to classify
the field rather than letting it default. Story 5.2's guarantee worked in the field, one story
later, with nobody attending to it. This is the pattern that works in this repository, and the
action items below are an attempt to apply it deliberately rather than occasionally.

**Decisions taken at drafting rather than deferred.** `mapping_confirmed` is a boolean and not a
fingerprint, and the reason is recorded: a fingerprint cannot be written by hand, and FR23
promises a headless bring-up. *A guard that makes the documented bring-up impossible is a second
bug, not a guard.* What replaces the fingerprint's self-invalidation is structural — the
withdrawal lives in `save`, at the boundary every writer passes, not in the screen that saved it.

**Recording the unmet criterion before anybody asked.** [#49] was opened the day story 5.2 was
written. That is the only reason 5.2 could be closed honestly on 2026-08-10 instead of sitting in
`review` indefinitely.

## What went wrong

### 1. A withheld DDEATH, reachable from the configuration screen

`classify_meters` inferred the served meter as *"the first enabled one"*. With four meters
running, disabling the second, third or fourth was classified `ProcessRestart`: **no DDEATH was
sent**, and the screen told the operator a restart would settle it. A Sparkplug host went on
displaying, as current, the last value of a meter the operator had just switched off.

This is the failure this project is named for, reached from the configuration screen instead of
from a failed poll. It was reachable on three of Guy's four meters.

Under the falsification that restored the inference, **eleven other tests stayed green** — every
one of them describes a one-meter bridge.

**The general lesson, which outlives the bug:** when a structural assumption falls — here "one
meter" became "four" — the tests that encoded it stay green and announce nothing. The fan-out
must be followed by an active search for what the old assumption was carrying, because the suite
will not raise its hand.

The repair is to stop inferring: the served set now comes from `Heartbeats::meters`, one entry
per spawned poll task. That is also the seam story 3.1 named as missing, and it is load-bearing
now — which is why [#63] exists.

### 2. The consequence does not travel — eighth occurrence

The code and the manual get corrected together; the acceptance criterion stating the consequence
does not. Story 5.3's AC3 stated the opposite of the shipped behaviour and was amended in place
during the closing review.

Eight occurrences is not a slip. It is a shape the process does not catch.

### 3. Hollow assertions, three in one file

Story 6.2's tests searched pages for strings the page contains regardless of the property under
test. `contains("in force now")` is printed by the change table on every hot row, so a mutation
making the verdict say the opposite left the test green. Underneath it, `needs_restart()` omitted
every `NewSession` field. Same family as `log.contains('3')` satisfied by the log's own RFC-3339
timestamp.

Fixing the assertion is what exposed the code — twice. That is worth stating positively: the
assertion audit is a defect-finding technique, not only a test-quality chore.

### 4. A box that cannot be audited

The `ci-local.sh` checkbox cannot be verified by reading. The honest substitute — the suite has
run over this tree since, and all three workflows are green on every later commit — is a
stronger claim, but it is not the claim the box makes.

## The Project Lead's reading, and it reframes the retrospective

Guy raised three observations. They are recorded in his terms because they are the finding, not a
comment on it.

**"A run of problems, and we circled them without resolving them."** Verifiable rather than
impressionistic: the repository *numbers its own repeats* — "fourth instance of one shape",
"fifth instance of the same class", "eighth occurrence of the recurring shape". A project that
has begun numbering its recurrences has observed that it is not stopping them.

The diagnosis is precise: **detection is excellent; conversion is what fails.** Each time, the
*instance* is repaired and the *class* is not. And this repository demonstrably knows how to do
the opposite — `arch_purity` and `classify`'s exhaustive destructure are mechanisms rather than
corrections, and neither has ever recurred. The skill exists; it is not being applied to the
shapes that are found.

**"Noting an issue in order to be able to close a story."** The rule *record it unmet, with an
issue* prevents silent closure, which is its purpose. But **it has no counterpart**: nothing ever
obliges anyone to act on the issue. The measured result is 65 items in `deferred-work.md` and 46
open issues, some from July.

This retrospective must record that **the facilitator did exactly this on 2026-08-10**: four
issues opened and seven stories closed in one movement, presented as "the condition is met". It
was met formally. In substance, six findings became four tracking lines and nothing moved closer
to resolution. ADR 0025 had already named the mechanism — *deferring to a deferred epic is how an
item stops being tracked* — and the diagnosis was never tooled.

**"Few retrospectives were run, and the experience not collected seems to have been missing."**
The count: Epic 0 `optional`, never run. Epic 1 `done`. Epics 3 and 4 `optional`, never run.
Epics 5 through 8 carried **no retrospective line at all** — the same omission that had left
epics 7 and 8 absent from the dashboard until 2026-08-08.

One retrospective, on 2026-07-26. Everything since — epics 4, 5, 6, half of 3 — ran without one.
**That is precisely the period in which tracking drifted**: the plan of record's execution order
was wrong for a month (ADR 0025), two epics were missing from the dashboard, and the recurring
shapes accumulated.

## The three are one finding

**This project diagnoses exceptionally well and almost never closes the loop.** A defect is found
→ it becomes an item → the item becomes an issue → the issue becomes a line in a file. Nothing in
the cycle returns to action. The retrospective is the instrument that closes that loop, and it
has been used once.

One share of this belongs to the facilitator and is recorded as such: **diagnostics are produced
faster than a single decision-maker can absorb them.** Sixty-five deferred items may be less a
failure of discipline than a production rate exceeding the available decision rate. Continuing to
produce findings without changing that will only lengthen the list.

## Decisions taken

1. **Epic 5 is functionally complete and may close.** FR43 and NFR16 are blocked upstream
   ([#50]) — `rumqttc` pins a `rustls-webpki` carrying unfixed advisories — which is a
   dependency's release, not a task anyone here can schedule. They are carried as *blocked
   upstream*, not as unstarted work.
2. **Story 5.2 closes with AC4's new-session half unmet**, carried by [#49].
3. **Retrospectives stop being optional.** The lines missing for epics 5 through 8 are added, and
   the epic 3 and epic 4 retrospectives are owed — Epic 4 especially, since it produced ADR 0025.

## What comes next, and the sequence is open

ADR 0025 set the forward order **6 → 3 → 2 → 7 → 8**. Three facts now sit against it and the
Project Lead has not been asked to re-decide:

- **Epic 6 has two stories written against twelve functional requirements** (FR28–FR38, FR44).
  Its server and configuration screen exist; its observability does not. [#62] lives there.
- **Epic 7 is what the deployment now lacks most.** The bridge runs in production, a SCADA reads
  it, and nothing watches the bridge itself: no healthcheck, and nothing inside the image can
  consume `/healthz` ([#56]).
- **Epic 2 still has no story.** "Never lies" rests on the freshness state machine alone. ADR
  0029 delivered one of its four oracles by accident this month, because a real fault demanded
  it.

On 2026-08-10 a real meter froze for over two hours, the wire reported it honestly as
`Bad_Stale`, and every operator surface reported the fleet healthy. That is Epic 6's unwritten
half and Epic 7's absence, measured on the running deployment rather than argued.

## Key takeaways

1. **The instance gets repaired; the class does not.** The repository's two mechanisms —
   `arch_purity`, the exhaustive destructure — never recurred. Everything corrected by hand
   recurred.
2. **When a structural assumption falls, the tests that encoded it stay green.** Eleven green
   one-meter tests hid a withheld DDEATH on three meters out of four.
3. **An issue without a named owner-story is a permit to close, not a debt.**
4. **A retrospective's value is measurable, and this one measures it**: five of six commitments
   honoured visibly changed the work; the sixth, unhonoured, produced the epic's main defect.
5. **Diagnosis is not the constraint here — decision is.** Producing findings faster than they
   can be acted on converts a strength into a backlog.
