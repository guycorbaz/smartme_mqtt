## What worked, and must be kept

- **Refusing machinery built on unobserved behaviour, three times in one day**: no
  listing-comparison loop (3.4 and again 3.5), no mistyped-vs-removed discrimination, no purge
  onto guessed topics (3.6). Each refusal produced something better — a fact-anchored
  mechanism, or an honestly reshaped requirement.
- **The arbitration pattern**: 3.6 was drafted to END in a decision, presented with costs and
  a recommendation, and Guy arbitrated the same day. A requirement changed without a line of
  speculative code — the FR14 precedent, matured into a working procedure.
- **Loop-level integration tests under virtual time**: 3.5's properties live in `run()`, and
  driving `run()` itself (start_paused, FakeClock advanced by hand) caught what `step_once`
  tests never could — including teaching the true kinetics of a hot period change.
- **Review convergence, again**: 3.4 took two rounds, 3.5 three, each smaller than the last;
  the final round found NO functional break, only missing proofs. The discipline's cost is
  real (three rounds is an afternoon) and its yield was eleven repaired defects, two of them
  security- or fleet-health-grade.
- **The screen's conventions as machine rules**: the four forbidden tokens, "client id" with
  spaces — one GitHub failure turned into a guard covering every future discovery surface.

## What went wrong

1. **A repair was pushed on lib tests and clippy alone** — the full gate had run for the story
   and was skipped for its repair; GitHub's AC6 word-scan was the first thing to read the
   page. The process lapse is named in `b538835`, and D1 below turns the rule into a
   mechanism.
2. **Repairs without pins** (3.5): the three largest behavioural repairs of the epic could
   each be reverted with the whole suite green. The reviewer's method — *revert the repair and
   watch the suite* — is cheap, mechanical, and now the standard (D2).
3. **Comments claiming "unreachable" that were false**: the missing-row arm's justification
   contradicted `Control::apply`'s actual behaviour, caught one round later. A claim of
   unreachability must cite the mechanism that enforces it, or not be made (D3).
4. **The wedge-on-idle defect shipped in the story commit and survived one round**: three
   independent finders converged on it in round two — evidence the multi-angle review earns
   its cost, and that a single-angle review would likely have missed the epic's gravest
   defect.

## Key takeaways

Epic 2 taught this repository how to test oracles; Epic 3 taught it how to test REPAIRS. The
three-round convergence on 3.5 — six repairs, then sound-mechanics-but-no-proofs, then pins —
is the whole arc in miniature: the code was right two rounds before it was PROVEN right, and
the difference between those states is exactly what a regression walks through. The epic's
other habit worth keeping cost nothing three times: when the honest design rests on behaviour
nobody has observed, the story says so and stops, and twice out of three the stopping WAS the
deliverable.
