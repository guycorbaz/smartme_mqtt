## The finding: this epic's instruments measured what their authors remembered

Every story in this epic shipped a **mechanism whose job was to check something**, and in every
story that mechanism was defective — always the same way.

| Instrument | What it measured instead | How it surfaced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8.1's route scan | `ui/mod.rs` **line by line**, so it missed every route whose path sits on the next line | Accused a correct manual of inventing `/config` and `/confirm`. Caught by its own presence assertion |
| 8.1's slug check (review) | One of the **two** cause vocabularies | Accused five real `DropReason` slugs — `outbox-full`, `transport-queue-full`, `mqtt-task-gone`, `before-birth`, `undeclared-device` — of being inventions |
| 8.2's purity guard | The literal string `prost::` on a `pub` line — the shape the defect had the day it was found | Two **ordinary** shapes walked past with the suite green: an aliased import, and a signature wrapped by rustfmt |
| 8.2's README | Nothing. It was written as a doctest and included nowhere | Three defects in the eight lines a consumer meets first on crates.io |
| The bridge gate's printed checklist | A contract version frozen at **3** while the binary emits **10**, and a step-4 question the Designer cannot answer | [#108], found by running it |
| The runbook's step 2 | Nothing it claims to: the gate stamps readings with `now`, so acquisition and reception fall in the same second | Found on 2026-08-22 by asking how the step could pass wrongly |
| The runbook's step 1 | A datatype readable neither in the Tag Browser nor in the Tag Editor | Found on 2026-08-22 by trying to read it |

And the day after 8.2 closed, the same question put to the whole repository — [#106] — found **five
of six mechanical guards blind** to the ordinary shape of their own fault.

**The instrument failed on the record, too.** The 2026-08-21 session notes said the MQTT Engine
version had never been supplied, *"third row in this table"* — while the version sat **two lines
above the claim, in the same table's 2026-08-03 row**. It was corrected on 2026-08-22, and then
asked of Guy again, minutes later, by the same hand that had just corrected it. Reading *about* the
record instead of reading it is the failure `CLAUDE.md` already names for the smart-me
specification; this epic proved it is not specific to that document.

## What Guy named, and what the record says about it

> *"Les tests ont été un peu fastidieux, mais cela semble fonctionner. De manière générale, j'ai eu
> l'impression de tourner un peu en rond avec Sparkplug."*

**The circling has a measurable shape, and it is not a feeling.** `CONTRACT_VERSION` stands at
**10**. It has been attested against a live host **twice**: at v3 on 2026-08-03, and at v10 today.
**Seven contract versions shipped without ever meeting a host** — v4's `Cause`, which this epic
finally measured, has been on the wire since Epic 2 and **has never reached an operator**. Sparkplug
conformance was worked in Epics 1, 2, 4 and 8; the audit that produced the conformance matrix is
Epic 4's, and [#101] says at least four of its 53 code citations no longer point at code. Two
positions had to be reversed after the fact: contract v1's quality codes, which a real host read as
`Good`, and ADR 0010's premise about QoS, amended by [#99].

**That is what tightening a contract against a specification instead of against a host feels like
from the inside.** The work was not wasted — every one of those versions is defensible on the wire —
but the loop that closes it is the human-run session, and it ran twice in four months.

**And the tedium is real and partly avoidable.** Two of the six steps could not measure what they
asked; one of them (step 2) needs a ~90-second change to the gate to become falsifiable.

## What worked, and must be kept

**The presence assertion.** Twice in one story, a scan was about to report a defect that did not
exist, and both times what caught it was the scan asserting *its own inputs looked sane* before
trusting its output — at least five routes, `/healthz` among them. **A scan that finds nothing and a
scan that is broken produce the same silence.**

**Allow-list over deny-list.** 8.2's rewritten guard names **what is ours** — `crate`, `self`,
`super`, `std`, `core`, `alloc`, the crate's own module stems, and `protobuf` by name — instead of
the five foreign crates its author happened to think of. A dependency added tomorrow cannot walk
past it. This is the single most transferable thing the epic produced.

**Mutations that live in a fixture.** A falsification performed once by hand proves a guard for the
day it was performed. 8.2's four shapes, plus one the guard must not flag, are re-run by CI.

**Compiling the document instead of reading it.** `#[cfg(doctest)] #[doc = include_str!("../README.md")]`
turns the README into something `cargo test` fails on, without putting its prose in the rendered
docs.

**Measuring the host's log instead of its browser.** Step 6 had never had a direct answer — only an
inference from a counter. One SQL query over the exported `.idb` gave three: both certificates
processed, no complaint from the Engine module in 3 h 10, and `Offline DateTime` tracking the will.
**The noise was measured rather than asserted**: 109 lines in the 25 seconds around the deaths, 9
`ERROR` and 32 `WARN`, none of them ours. Scrolling would have produced a confident false failure.

## What went wrong

**1. Every guard this epic shipped was written against the defect's remembered form.** Detailed
above. The cost was not a shipped defect — all were caught — but each one had to be found twice.

**2. A requirement was satisfied in a different container and nobody wrote that down.** AR16 and
AR20 name nine markdown documents and eight ADRs; the substance lives in eleven LaTeX chapters and
thirty-four ADRs. Second occurrence of [#89]'s shape.

**3. The human-run gate carried a stale claim into the operator's hands.** [#108]: the printed
checklist announced `Contract/Version = 3` for seven versions. Nothing re-reads a printed checklist
when the constant it quotes moves.

**4. The contract advanced seven versions between attestations**, and the one requirement that
cannot be discharged alone was the last thing the project did.

## Readiness assessment

**Epic 8 is closable, and with it the roadmap.** All three stories are done, every FR and NFR it
carries has a named artifact, and AR21 is met as written. What it does not attest is written in
story 8.3's AC3 rather than dressed up: the per-metric verdict of contract v6 has still never
diverged on a wire a host read, and [#107]'s measurement has no positive control.

**Guy's decision, taken at this retrospective: go to production now.**

That decision closes a window this project has been spending freely, and `CLAUDE.md` names it:
*"the bridge is not yet in production and no tag historisation has begun, so wire-breaking changes
are currently cheap. That window closes without announcing itself."* Two open items need the window
and only the window:

- **[#100] — the first `bdSeq` a new bridge publishes is 1, and `tck-id-topics-nbirth-bdseq-increment`
  requires it to start at zero.** A wire-visible conformance defect, measured rather than deduced.
- **[#107] — contract v4's `Cause` does not reach the operator.** Both candidate repairs change the
  wire: declaring `Cause` at BIRTH, or carrying the cause as a metric. And the experiment that
  chooses between them is the arbitration story 2.1's task 3 has been blocking since Epic 2.

Deciding these **after** the first historised tag exists costs a retype of history; deciding them
this week costs nothing. That is the whole of the recommendation — the arbitration is Guy's.

**The crate is not published, and should not be.** Guy's reading, and the record agrees with it: a
crates.io version cannot be withdrawn, only yanked; `sparkplug-b` is exercised almost entirely
*through the bridge*, and **nothing feeds `decode` hostile bytes** — truncated, random, or a valid
protobuf that is not this message — which is exactly where a stranger arrives; and the conformance
scope the README advertises rests on a matrix with at least four stale citations ([#101]). The
README example, compiled since 8.2's review, is the one outside-consumer test that exists.

## Key takeaways

- **An instrument written from a defect measures that defect.** Six instruments, one epic, and the
  distinguishing feature of the healthy one was that it read a live source instead of a remembered
  list.
- **A scan's silence is ambiguous.** It found nothing, or it is broken, and only an assertion about
  its own inputs tells them apart.
- **Seven contract versions between two host attestations is what "going in circles" looks like from
  inside.** The specification can be read alone; the host cannot be.
- **The record is an instrument too**, and this epic misread it twice in two days — including once
  immediately after correcting it.
