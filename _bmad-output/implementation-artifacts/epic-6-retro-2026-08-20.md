## What worked, and must be kept

**Ordering 6.3 before 6.4 was the whole reason the screens are honest.** AR19 says the UI
consumes state and never recomputes it. Building the state first meant the meter page could not
quietly become the second place the truth lives — and story 6.6 then inherited the same
discipline and refused to judge the reading it fetches, for a reason that only became visible
by reading `step_once`: the oracles judge against a per-meter memory a button must not move.

**Deciding at drafting, twice, and both times it changed the work.**

- 6.6 decided *before any code* that the check would report three facts from three owners
  rather than re-judge anything. An implementation that had discovered this later would have
  shipped a second assembly of judgements.
- 6.7 could not decide alone: FR35 names "priority meters" and nothing in the model, or in
  smart-me's payload, knows what that is. **Guy decided** — the flag became the operator's own
  mark. The alternative that was refused (deduce it from the meter name) is exactly the
  invented-fact failure this repository's rules name first.

**Falsification found real defects, not just green tests.** Every story this session ran its
mutations, and three of them produced a defect rather than a confirmation: a topic rendered as
`spBv1.0/G/DDATA/N/—` for a meter with no serial (6.4), a page still *saying* it refreshes
itself after the refresh was removed (6.6), and *"created just now"* about a file planted
seconds earlier (6.7). **The dumps did the work** — an assertion that only said "wrong" would
have left all three.

**Clippy improved the design twice, and neither time was it wrong.** `record_at`'s eight
arguments became `Publication`, which is the pairing story 6.3 AC1 exists to keep together; a
ten-element tuple of borrows became `SettingsView`.

## What went wrong

**1. Three acceptance criteria were ticked against a summary of themselves.** This is the
epic's signature defect and it recurred in three different shapes:

- **6.4 AC2** lists nine columns; eight shipped, and the completion note listed the eight as
  though they were the nine. The missing one was the one FR28 names by that name — the
  measurement's age. Story 6.3 had stored `source_value_date` and `staleness_threshold_ms` for
  it, and **nobody read either field** until the review.
- **6.5 AC3** says *"the state screen"*. The sink line shipped on `/meters`, and the note
  recorded AC3 as met while naming the other surface. `/` went on saying the broker's
  reachability was "not reported here yet" — a sentence made false by the same commit.
- **6.8 AC2**'s own test admitted `"the"` as a valid opening, which let any declarative
  sentence through. The assertion still ran and could no longer fail for its own reason.

The common mechanism: **the note compares the work against the summary it has just written,
not against the criterion.** That is action A6 ([#24]) — *verify each AC actually covers what
it claims* — unhonoured for the third epic running, and Epic 5's retrospective predicted this
exact failure.

**2. Two delivered requirements were counted as owed.** FR46 was written into the PRD on
2026-08-03 and into both epics' scope lines, and **never into the FR coverage map** — the one
place coverage is measured. FR38 has been delivered since 2026-08-01 by `main.rs`, claimed by
no story at all. Epic 6 looked like it owed four requirements when it owed two.

**3. An ADR asserted a technical consequence it had not measured, and the implementation
falsified it.** ADR 0039 said the three new fields being `#[serde(default)]` was enough for an
existing file to read. The version check lives in `read` — which pre-fills the configuration
screen — so a version-4 file would have been refused everywhere and the operator would have
found an empty form. That is FR27 failing through the back door, caught only because the
implementation went looking. ADR 0040 (the first migration) exists because of it.

**4. [#101] recurs at every opportunity, and now it has a number.** Adding **one line** to
`epics.md` invalidated **26 citations** across eighteen stories, `deferred-work.md` and two
ADRs. Three more were displaced by this session's own code edits. E4 catches them; nothing
stops them being made.

## Decisions this epic leaves on the table **[Guy]**

- **Cite planning documents by anchor rather than by line.** The measurement is on [#101]: the
  exposure is not four drifted citations, it is that every insertion moves every citation below
  it. Anchors (a heading, an FR id) are stable and just as checkable.
- **The context line renders ages, not calendar dates.** *"Created three weeks ago"* answers
  "is this recent?" and not "which day?". An absolute date needs a date library among the
  direct dependencies, which is a dependency decision and wants its own ADR.
- **[#77] is still open and story 6.6 walked past it deliberately.** A 429 on the token
  endpoint arms no wait; the check's rate rule keeps the button from causing one, and does not
  fix the gap.

## Readiness assessment

**Epic 6 is closable.** Eight stories settled, thirteen FRs covered — FR46 and FR38 by claim as
well as by code now — and every defect the reviews found was repaired inside the epic rather
than deferred. Two issues were opened and one closed on the day, and both new ones ([#104],
[#105]) carry decisions Guy took rather than findings left hanging.

**What the epic did not change is the bridge's readiness**, and that is worth saying plainly:
this was an epic about surfaces. The jalon that matters — the Tier-3 session against a live
Ignition, still attested only at contract v3 on 2026-08-03 while the bridge emits v10 — has not
moved, and nothing in Epic 6 could move it.

**The one thing not to carry forward unexamined is F1.** Three epics have now recorded the same
action about acceptance criteria, and the defect arrived three more times this epic. An
intention repeated across three retrospectives is not a process; it is a wish. F1 makes it a
sentence somebody has to type.

## Key takeaways

- **The state before the screen.** Ordering 6.3 first is why nothing on these pages judges
  anything, and why 6.6 could refuse to.
- **A criterion you re-read is worth three you summarise.** Every AC defect this epic was found
  by reading the criterion again, and every one of them had passed a completion note.
- **Measure the consequence you are about to write into an ADR.** ADR 0039's false claim cost
  nothing only because the implementation checked; it would have cost a full retype in
  production.
- **The dump is the assertion.** Three defects this epic were found because the failure message
  printed what was rendered, not because a test said "wrong".
